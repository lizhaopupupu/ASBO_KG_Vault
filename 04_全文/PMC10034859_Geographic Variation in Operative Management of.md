## Background:

Variation in surgical management exists nationally. We hypothesize that geographic variation exists in adhesive small bowel obstruction (aSBO) management.

## Materials and Methods:

A retrospective analysis of a national commercial insurance claims database (MarketScan) sample (2017–2019) was performed in adults with hospital admission due to aSBO. Geographic variation in rates of surgical intervention for aSBO was evaluated by state and compared to a risk adjusted national baseline using a Bayesian spatial rates Poisson regression model. For individual-level analysis, patients were identified in 2018, with 365-day look-back and follow up periods. Logistic regression was performed for individual-level predictors of operative intervention for aSBO.

## Results:

Two thousand one hundred forty-five patients were included. State level analysis revealed rates of operative intervention for aSBO were significantly higher in Missouri and lower in Florida. On individual-level analysis, age (p<0.01) and male sex (p<0.03), but not comorbidity profile or prior aSBO, were negatively associated with undergoing operative management for aSBO. Patients presenting in 2018 with a history of admission for aSBO the year prior experienced a five-fold increase in odds of re-presentation (OR: 5.4, 95% CI: 3.1–9.6) in 2019. Patients who received an operation for aSBO in 2018 reduced the odds of readmission in the next year by 77% (OR: 0.23, 95% CI: 0.1–0.5). The volume of operations performed within a state did not influence readmission.

## Conclusions:

Surgical management of aSBO varies across the continental USA. Operative intervention is associated with decreased rates of re-presentation in the following year. These data highlight a critical need for standardized guidelines for EGS patients.

## Introduction

Small bowel obstruction due to adhesions (aSBO) is a common diagnosis within the Emergency General Surgery (EGS) patient population, resulting in over 250,000 admissions annually, and is a major driver of hospital costs and readmissions.1–3 The gold standard for treatment is surgical division of the intra-abdominal scar tissue (adhesiolysis).4 In patients presenting with inadequate blood flow to the bowel or bowel perforation due to aSBO, immediate surgical management is imperative. However, a non-operative approach with bowel rest and symptom management is frequently undertaken in the absence of these factors with successful resolution of aSBO in approximately 70% of cases.4 In this group of patients, the timing and rationale to proceed with surgical care is variable among surgeons and hospitals, as operation is considered a failure of medical management.4,5

Across surgical specialties, variation in performance of operative procedures is common by region of the country.6–8 Previous work has shown that geographic residence is often more predictive of undergoing an operation than clinical context.8 Other factors, such as patient demand, physician beliefs, and incorporation of patient preferences, beyond differences in illness burden and practice guidelines, are potential drivers for performance of an operation.8,9 Recognizing the disease heterogeneity of aSBO, we hypothesize that geographic variation exists across the continental United States in the performance of operative management. Furthermore, these differences in practice may affect readmission. To analyze this, we retrospectively compared rates of surgical intervention for aSBO by state using a national commercial insurance claims database covering >25 million individuals aged <65 years.

## Materials and methods

## Setting and Design

We performed a population-based retrospective cohort study to evaluate the state-level variation in management and outcomes of patients with aSBO in the continental United States of America (USA).

## Data Source

Data were collected through the IBM Marketscan Research Databases, a national commercial insurance claims database covering >25 million individuals aged <65 years, employed by >300 self-insured employers and representing >25 US health plans.10 Health information for employees (primary insured individuals) and family members, as well as dependents, are included in 4 databases: demographic records, inpatient medical records, outpatient medical records, and outpatient prescription records. Specific data points included are age, sex, index condition, procedures performed, comorbid conditions, prescription medications, discharge status, geography, and provider information (i.e., hospitals, physicians). Mortality is not an available data point in the database. The data have been previously collected and de-identified, thus are Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) compliant. Institutional Review Board approval was additionally obtained (IRB00075966).

## Cohort

We identified a cohort of patients aged 18–64 years who were admitted to hospital for aSBO from 2017–2019 in the continental USA. Patients were identified within the sample by the following International Classification of Diseases 10th edition (ICD-10) diagnosis codes for intestinal adhesions with obstruction (K56.50), intestinal adhesions with partial obstruction (K56.51), and intestinal adhesions with complete obstruction (K56.52). Patients were excluded who experienced bowel obstruction due to other causes or were lost to follow up due to death or changes in insurance coverage. The following were excluded ICD-10 diagnosis codes: K56.2 (diagnosis of volvulus); K40.x-K46.x (diagnosis of hernia); K56.0, K56.7 (diagnosis of paralytic ileus or ileus, unspecified); K56.1 (diagnosis of intussusception); K56.3 (diagnosis of gallstone ileus); K56.4 (diagnosis of impaction of intestine); K50, K51 (diagnosis of inflammatory bowel disease); C16–18.x C19, C20, C21.x, C26.0, C54.x, C56.x, C61.x, C67.x, C76.2, C76.3, C76.7, C76.8 (diagnosis of abdominal malignancy); C77.2, C78.4, C78.5, C78.6, C78.8, C79.0, C79.1, C79.6, C79.7, C79.8, C80.x (carcinomatosis). This method for identification of patients with aSBO is similarly described in the contemporary literature.11,12 Patients were uniquely identified in 2018 with a 1 year (365 days) look-back (pre-index) period in 2017 for comorbidity characterization. Patients with multiple hospitalizations were included as separate data points. Patients undergoing operative intervention for aSBO were identified by the following Current Procedural Terminology (CPT) codes: 44180 (laparoscopy, surgical with enterolysis), 44005 (enterolysis, freeing of intestinal adhesion).

## Outcomes

Our primary outcome measure is the rate of surgical intervention for aSBO by state in the continental USA. Secondary outcomes include rates of readmission in the following year after index presentation in 2018. We also explored the relationship between readmission in the following year in patients who underwent operation for aSBO and volume of aSBO operations performed in a state.

## Covariates

We identified individual and state-level covariates that were potential confounders to include in our Poisson regression model. Individual covariates were pre-index patient characteristics observed the year (365 days) prior to admission in 2018 for aSBO such as age, sex, prior admission for aSBO and patient comorbidities from the weighted Elixhauser comorbidity categories.13,14 The weighted Elixhauser score is a validated modification of the 30 binomial variable Elixhauser comorbidity score into a single numeric score for the purposes of comparing administrative datasets. State-level covariates included state population, population density, number of surgeons per state, number of surgeons per 100,000 population, number of hospitals per state, geography of a hospital (urban versus rural), and number of Accreditation Council for Graduate Medical Education (ACGME) affiliated institutions per state to represent the number of surgical training programs per state.

## Analytic Approach

Geographic variation in rates of surgical intervention for aSBO was evaluated by state and compared to a risk adjusted national baseline using a Bayesian spatial rates Poisson regression model.15,16 A probability of operation was estimated for each patient based on their pre-index characteristics and those probabilities were summed within a state to give the expected number. We computed posterior probabilities of the probability that a state operates more than expected. To both account for spatial dependence and help to stabilize estimates from states with small counts, we included a spatial random effect using a conditional autoregressive model.17 Similarly, Alaska and Hawaii were excluded due to small counts and the absence of a nearest neighbor for spatial correlation. Risk-adjusted outcomes were expressed as an observed-to-expected (O/E) operative rate ratio by state, as an application of previously described methodologies by the senior author.18,19 We plotted each state’s standardized rate ratio and 95% credible interval, or certainty, after controlling for comorbidities to evaluate for significant differences. Logistic regression was performed for individual-level predictors of operative intervention for aSBO. We subsequently evaluated each state for rates of readmission the following year based upon operative volumes for aSBO.

We performed descriptive statistics for assessment of baseline characteristics, including means with standard deviations (SD) for continuous variables with categorical variables expressed as counts with percentages. Analyses were performed using R statistical software using the NIMBLE package and SAS Studio (SAS Institute, Cary, NC).20 Statistical significance was set a priori at p<0.05 (two-sided).

## Results

Over the study period, a total of 2,145 patients, identified in 2018, were included in our sample. Overall, 23% (n=500) of patients were managed operatively. Demographics of operative versus non-operative patients are presented in Table 1. Patients managed with operation were younger (p<0.01) and more likely to be female (p<0.03). Comorbidity profile, evidence of prior aSBO and state-level covariates did not demonstrate a significant association with odds of operative intervention in our sample. A total 4% (n=90) of patients admitted with aSBO in 2018 were re-admitted the following year with this diagnosis. For each patient identified in 2018, a history of admission for aSBO in 2017 (within 365 days prior to 2018 admission), independent of management strategy at that time, experienced a five-fold increase in odds of readmission (OR: 5.4, 95% CI: 3.1–9.6) in 2019. However, patients who received an operation for aSBO in 2018 reduced the odds of readmission for aSBO in the next year by 77% (OR: 0.23, 95% CI: 0.1–0.5). The volume of operations performed within a state for aSBO did not influence readmission for patients who underwent surgical management of aSBO.
