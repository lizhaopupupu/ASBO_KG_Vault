# Management of small bowel obstruction and therapeutic role of contrast media: A multicenter study.

> PMC 开放获取全文（PMC11749962），对应文献笔记：[[39845293_Management of small bowel obstruction and therapeutic role]]

## Background

Adhesive small bowel obstruction (SBO) accounts for 55–75% of mechanical small bowel obstructions [1,2]. According to guidelines, water-soluble contrast media (CM) is suggested in the diagnostic work-up of adhesive SBO [3], [4], [5]. However, there is currently no consensus on the therapeutic role of water-soluble CM in adhesive SBO [6] or a generally approved protocol as to when CM should be administered, how often should radiographs be taken and how long conservative management can safely be pursued.

## Material and methods

The data of patients who were hospitalized for SBO in two regional hospitals of Estonia between January 2015 and December 2019 were retrospectively collected from the hospitals’ electronic databases.

## Results

Of 1,008 patients with mechanical SBO included in analysis, 140 (13.9%) were operated on immediately. In the case of conservative management, 575 (66.2%) patients received CM. Resolution occurred in 75.8% of the patients who received CM and in 68.9% of the patients who did not (p = 0.037). Resolution rate was the highest (78.8%) in the group that received CM within 12 h of admission. The duration of symptoms was longer in patients who needed surgical intervention compared to conservatively treated patients (31 h vs 15 h, p<0.001). For patients with a history of a single previous abdominal/pelvic operation, the likelihood of surgical intervention was higher compared to patients whom resolution was achieved with conservative measures (50.9% vs 38.5%). In patients with three or more previous operations SBO resolved more often with conservative treatment compared to patients who required surgical intervention (22.7% vs 11.4%; p<0.001).

## Conclusion

The significantly higher rate of SBO resolution in the CM group suggests that CM may have a therapeutic role in SBO treatment, especially when it is administered within 12 h of admission. Patients with longer duration of symptoms and a history of one previous abdominal surgery need more likely surgical treatment.

## Keywords

## Introduction

Despite being an actual topic of numerous research papers, consensus on the role of water-soluble contrast media (CM) in management of SBO, as well as a unified protocol of how to guide its treatment are still lacking. Mechanical SBO is a condition that requires prompt evaluation and management. Adhesions are the most common cause of SBO [7]. Patient's primary evaluation should be conducted and the surgeon has to opt for a treatment strategy. Computed tomography (CT) is the recommended imaging technique, as it is more informative than plain radiograph [4]. Rebound tenderness and abdominal guarding are late signs [8] and, when associated with elevated C- reactive protein (CRP) >75 mg/L and white blood cell (WBC) count >10×109 /L, alludes to abdominal emergency [9] and suggests the possible need for surgical exploration.

The Bologna guidelines suggest use of water-soluble CM as part of the management algorithm in the case of adhesive small bowel obstruction [4]. According to several studies, water-soluble CM has a therapeutic effect due to its hyper-osmolar qualities that reduce bowel wall edema and help restore bowel motility [10], [11], [12], [13]. Koh et al., on the other hand, concludes that there remains a lack of evidence that water-soluble CM offers therapeutic benefit on its own. Despite the variation of conducted studies, there is no general consensus as to when water-soluble CM should be administered, nor are there recommendations about the timing of the decision to operate [6].

The main aim of the present multicenter retrospective study was to evaluate the association of CM on resolution of SBO. Another aim was to find predictive factors that could suggest the need for earlier operative management or predict the failure of conservative management.

## Material and methods

The study was approved by the Research Ethics Committee of the University of Tartu.

This is a multicenter retrospective study conducted at two regional hospitals of Estonia: Tartu University Hospital and North Estonian Medical Center. The data of the patients’ hospitalized for SBO between January 2015 and December 2019 were collected from electronic databases.

Eligible patients were adults aged 18 years or older with mechanical small bowel obstruction. Patients younger than 18 years, patients with large bowel obstruction or dynamic small bowel obstruction and/or patients who had undergone abdominal/pelvic surgery within 4 weeks of admission were excluded from the study. Patients diagnosed with incarcerated abdominal wall hernia were also excluded from analysis.

Based on the International Classification of Diseases 10th revision (ICD-10) codes K56.0-K56.7, patients with bowel obstruction were identified from electronic databases. All the case history and data were expatiated and recorded by the corresponding author and thus the inconsistent interpretation of data should be eliminated. The duration of symptoms (from symptom onset to the time of presentation to the emergency department) was documented. Characteristics of abdominal pain (absent/intermittent/permanent), presence of nausea/vomiting, absence of flatus, results of blood tests (white blood cell count (WBC), C-reactive protein (CRP), lactate level (Lac), estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR)), radiology imaging techniques used, previous abdominal/pelvic surgeries, previous episodes of bowel obstruction. It was documented whether immediate surgical treatment was implemented, or whether conservative management was initiated. In the case of conservative treatment, it was recorded whether contrast media (CM) was or was not used. In the case of surgical treatment, the type of surgery (adhesiolysis/ bowel resection/ other) was recorded.

The software package Statistica version 13.3 (TIBCO Software, California, USA) was used for statistical calculations. Categorical variables were given as count and percentages, comparison between the study groups was made using Pearson's Chi square test or Fischer's exact test. Continuous data were presented as means with standard deviation (SD) for normally distributed variables, or medians with 25% and 75% percentiles in the case of a non-normal distribution. When comparing two groups (operative vs conservative treatment) Student´s t-test was used for the means and nonparametric Mann-Whitney U test was used for non-normally distributed variables. ANOVA and non-parametric Kruskal-Wallis tests were employed when three different conservative treatment study groups were compared. All statistical tests were two-sided and p< 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Of the 1008 patients included in the analysis, 140 were operated within 6 h of admission. The remaining 868 were managed conservatively (nil per mouth, infusion therapy, analgetics) (Fig. 1). A nasogastric tube was inserted in 697 patients as part of conservative treatment, 159 were left without it and in 12 cases the data was missing.

In the group of conservative treatment 66.2% of patients were given CM (water-soluble CM in 561 cases, Barium in 14 cases). Altogether 391 patients received CM within 12 h of admission and 184 patients received it at a later time (from 13 to 120 h; median time 21 h). In 33.8% of the cases the contrast media challenge was not commenced (Fig. 1). There were no predetermined protocols as to how many radiographs are to be made and what should be the interval between them.

Out of the 293 patients who did not receive contrast media, SBO resolved in 68.9% of the cases. Of the patients who were given CM within 12 h of admission 78.8% had resolution. In the patient group who received CM later than 12 h of hospitalization, resolution occurred in 69.6% (Fig. 1). Overall, resolution occurred in 75.8% of the patients who received CM and in 68.9% of those who did not receive CM (p = 0.037).
